Five days on the fjords

Western NorwayJune 20246 min read

Mist at dawn, ferries for roads, and the light that never quite leaves in June.

I went to the fjords for the quiet. Five days, one rental car, a ferry timetable folded into my jacket, and no plan beyond driving north until the road ran out. The first morning I woke before the alarm, walked down to the water, and understood why people come back here for the rest of their lives.

The light in June never quite leaves. At two in the morning the sky is a soft grey-blue, and by four it is already gold on the peaks. You stop keeping time by the sun and start keeping it by the ferries.

The ferry north

The road along the Sognefjord is narrow, slow and worth every minute. Each bend opens on another wall of rock dropping into green water, and every few kilometres there is a lay-by where someone has stopped, just to look. The tourist board calls it one of the most beautiful drives in the world, and for once that is an understatement.

The ferries are part of the road here. You roll on, buy a coffee, stand at the rail, and roll off twenty minutes later on the other side of the water.

By the third day I had stopped reaching for the camera at every viewpoint. The good photos came when I waited: for a cloud to move, for a boat to cross, for the wind to drop.

The falls were louder than I expected. You hear them long before you see them, and then you feel them on your face.

Small practical notes

  • Ferries run late in summer, but the last one still leaves earlier than you think.
  • Bring a proper rain shell, even when the forecast says sun.
  • Fuel up whenever you can; stations are far apart north of Sogndal.

The mountains don't care whether you photograph them. That is the whole point.

On the last morning I drove back the way I had come, in the opposite light. Three things I would do again:

  1. Leave the itinerary loose.
  2. Take the slow road, every time.
  3. Stay up for the two o'clock light at least once.

I will be back. Next time, further north, and later in the year, when the light finally does leave and the sky starts to move.
